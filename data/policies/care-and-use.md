# Care and use guide

_Tom's Kitchen customer policy handbook · Section 7 · Last updated 2026-09-01_

Looking after your kitchenware properly keeps it working longer, and it's also what the warranty (Section 3) and the Cook With It Guarantee (1.2) expect. Each product in the catalog also has its own short care note; where the two differ, the product's note wins.

## 7.1 Nonstick cookware

- Use wood, silicone or nylon utensils. Metal utensils scratch the coating, and scratches aren't covered by the warranty.
- Cook on low to medium heat. Nonstick doesn't need high heat, and heating an empty nonstick pan on high can damage the coating in minutes.
- Oven safe to the temperature listed for each pan (most of ours to 400°F). Lids with vents are oven safe to 350°F.
- Even where a pan is listed as dishwasher safe, hand washing makes the coating last longer. Don't use abrasive pads or scouring powder.
- Cooking sprays leave a sticky residue that builds up over time. Use a little oil or butter instead.
- Our nonstick pans are **not induction compatible** unless the catalog says so. Induction-ready nonstick pans have a steel plate in the base and are marked "induction" in the listing.

## 7.2 Stainless steel (tri-ply and clad)

- Preheat the pan for 1–2 minutes before adding oil, then add food once the oil shimmers. This is what stops food from sticking.
- Rainbow tints and white spots are harmless. Remove them with a splash of white vinegar or a stainless steel cleanser.
- Tri-ply stainless is dishwasher safe and oven safe to 500°F, including lids unless they have glass or silicone parts.
- All of our tri-ply stainless steel works on **induction**.

## 7.3 Enameled cast iron

- Heat gradually on low to medium; enameled cast iron holds heat so well that high heat is rarely needed.
- Never put cold water into a hot pot. Thermal shock can crack the enamel, and that isn't covered.
- Hand wash only. The dishwasher dulls the enamel's finish over time.
- Oven safe to 500°F; the knob on the lid is oven safe to 450°F.
- Works on every hob, including induction. Lift, don't slide, on glass and induction hobs.
- These pots are heavy (a 7 qt Dutch oven weighs about 15 lb empty). Use two hands and oven gloves.

## 7.4 Carbon steel and cast iron (bare)

- Arrives with a light factory seasoning. The color will darken, blotch and turn bronze or black as you cook. That is normal and expected.
- Wash with hot water and a brush; a little soap is fine. Dry straight away over low heat, then wipe with a thin layer of neutral oil.
- **Never** put it in the dishwasher or leave it soaking. It will rust.
- If rust appears, scrub it off with steel wool, rinse, dry and re-season: thin coat of oil, 1 hour in a 450°F oven upside down.
- Works on gas, electric and induction. Avoid acidic dishes like tomato sauce until the seasoning has built up.

## 7.5 Knives

- Hand wash and dry straight away. Never put knives in the dishwasher; the heat and knocking damage the edge and the handle.
- Cut on wood, bamboo or plastic boards. Glass, stone and ceramic boards dull the edge fast.
- Don't cut bone or frozen food with chef's knives or paring knives.
- Hone every few uses and sharpen once or twice a year. Our whetstone or our mail-in sharpening service (3.5) both work.
- Store in a block, on a magnetic strip or with edge guards, not loose in a drawer.

## 7.6 Wooden boards and utensils

- Hand wash with warm soapy water and dry standing on edge. Never soak or put in the dishwasher.
- Oil with food-grade mineral oil or board cream about **once a month**, or when the wood looks dry.
- Light scratches can be sanded out with fine sandpaper, then oiled.
- To remove smells, rub with coarse salt and half a lemon.

## 7.7 Small appliances

- Built for **120 V, 60 Hz** power only (see 3.3). Don't use with voltage converters.
- Descale kettles and espresso machines monthly in hard-water areas: fill with equal parts water and white vinegar (or our descaling solution), run, then rinse by running twice with clean water.
- Never immerse the base of a kettle, blender, mixer or rice cooker in water.
- Air fryer baskets and blender jars are dishwasher safe where the listing says so; bases and motors are not.
- Keep appliances at least 5 in from walls and cabinets while running so they can vent.

## 7.8 Glass and ceramics

- Borosilicate glass containers go from freezer to oven, but let them stand at room temperature for 10 minutes first and put them into a preheated oven, not a cold one that heats up. Lids are not oven safe.
- Don't put hot glass on a wet or cold surface.
- Ceramic drippers and mugs are dishwasher safe unless they have wood or metal parts.

## 7.9 Silicone

- Food-grade silicone is safe from -40°F to 450°F and in the microwave, freezer and dishwasher.
- If silicone picks up smells, bake it empty at 250°F for 30 minutes.
- Don't cut on silicone mats or use sharp tools inside silicone bags.
