# Support playbook

_Tom's Kitchen customer policy handbook · Section 9 · Internal: for support agents, human and automated · Last updated 2026-09-01_

This section is for the people and assistants who answer customers. Sections 1–8 say what the policies are. This section says how to apply them: what you can decide on your own, what needs a supervisor, and what must go to a specialist team.

## 9.1 Principles

1. **Answer the question that was asked, then the one behind it.** A customer asking "how do returns work?" about a Dutch oven also wants to know about the surcharge and the label fee. Tell them both.
2. **Be exact.** Quote the real fee, the real date and the real SKU. "A small fee" or "a few weeks" creates a second contact.
3. **Say what you'll do and when.** "I've sent a return label to your email; your refund will be issued within 3 business days of the box reaching us" beats "we'll sort it."
4. **Never promise something the policy doesn't allow** to end a conversation faster. If an exception seems fair, use the goodwill rules in 9.4 or escalate.
5. **Ask for the minimum.** If you can find the order from an email address, don't ask for the order number as well.

## 9.2 Identifying the order

- Order numbers look like **TK-10201**. Customers often drop the "TK-", add spaces or a "#", or swap two digits. Tracking numbers also start with "TK" but are much longer. Don't confuse the two.
- If an order number doesn't match any order, check for swapped digits and match on the customer's name and email before asking again.
- Never ask a customer for a full card number, security code or password (5.6). To confirm identity on billing questions, the email on the order plus the last four digits of the card is enough.

## 9.3 What you can decide on your own

Any agent can do these without approval:

| Action | Limit |
|---|---|
| Issue a return label and authorize a return that meets Section 1 | Any value |
| Approve a warranty claim that meets Section 3, with a photo | Replacement or replacement part of any value; refunds up to **$300** |
| Send a replacement for an item damaged in transit (2.9) | Any value, once per order |
| Reship or refund a lost package (2.8) | Up to **$300** per order |
| Apply a forgotten promo code as a refund (4.3) | Within the code's own rules |
| Price adjustment (4.2) | Within the policy's rules |
| Goodwill store credit (9.4) | Up to **$25** per customer per 12 months |
| Waive a return label fee (9.4) | Once per customer per 12 months |

Anything above these limits needs a **support supervisor**.

## 9.4 Goodwill

Goodwill is for when we got something wrong that the policies don't fully fix: a slow reply, a delivery that missed an occasion, a wrong answer from us earlier in the conversation.

- Offer **store credit up to $25**, or **waive one return label fee**. Not both, and not every time.
- Goodwill is never paid to a card and never offered as cash.
- Goodwill can't turn a final sale item into a returnable one, or extend a window by more than **7 days**. Anything more than 7 days is a supervisor decision.
- Don't offer goodwill for things outside our control, such as carrier weather delays, unless the customer also had a poor experience with us.
- Record every goodwill gesture on the customer's account with a one-line reason.

## 9.5 When to escalate

Escalate to a **support supervisor** (same business day) when:

- the customer asks for something above your limits in 9.3,
- the customer asks for an exception to a written policy,
- the customer has contacted us three or more times about the same problem, or
- the customer asks for a manager.

Hand straight to the **billing team** (within 1 business hour) when:

- the customer reports a duplicate or unexpected charge that is **more than 5 business days old** (earlier than that, explain authorization holds first, 5.3),
- the customer mentions a **chargeback** or **dispute** with their bank, or
- a refund issued more than 10 business days ago hasn't arrived.

Hand straight to the **product safety team** (same business day) when:

- a product has caused an **injury**, burn or cut beyond what normal use would explain,
- a product sparked, smoked, melted or caught fire, or
- a customer describes a hazard that may affect other units. Known issue KI-001 (kettle lid) is already being handled; follow 3.7 and escalate only if someone was hurt.

A customer finding a product heavier or harder to use than they expected is **not** a safety escalation. Help them with a return or a lighter alternative.

Hand to the **legal team** only when a customer mentions a lawyer, a lawsuit, a regulator or a media story. Don't discuss the complaint further; acknowledge it and tell them who will contact them and when.

## 9.6 Common situations

**The customer is angry.** Acknowledge the specific problem in one sentence, then move straight to the fix. Don't argue about whose fault it was. Don't use the word "unfortunately" more than once.

**The customer is wrong about a policy.** Explain the actual policy briefly and politely, then offer what they *can* have. For example, if they want a cash refund on a used skillet after 45 days, offer store credit under the Cook With It Guarantee.

**The customer bought from another retailer.** Warranty claims are fine with proof of purchase (3.1). Returns and price adjustments must go through the retailer they bought from (1.13).

**The customer wants to know when something is back in stock.** Give the restock date from the catalog as an estimate, suggest the "Email me when it's back" button or a back-order, and don't promise the date.

**The customer is moving abroad.** Cookware, knives and storage are fine anywhere. Appliances are 120 V only and a voltage converter voids the warranty (3.3). We can't ship to the new address (2.1).

**The customer asks for a discount.** Mention WELCOME10 only if they haven't ordered before (4.4), and the verified discounts if they might qualify (4.5). Don't create one-off codes.

**The customer asks about a competitor's price.** We don't price match other retailers (4.2). Don't criticize the competitor.

**The customer asks something the handbook doesn't cover.** Say you'll find out, escalate to a supervisor, and tell the customer when they'll hear back. Never guess at a policy.

## 9.7 Tone

Friendly, direct and plain. Short sentences. Use the customer's name once. No exclamation marks in apologies. Sign off with your first name only; automated assistants sign off as "Tom's Kitchen Support".

## 9.8 Response time targets

| Channel | First reply | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Chat | 2 minutes | Same conversation where possible |
| Phone | 3 minutes on hold | Same call where possible |
| Email | 1 business day | 3 business days |
| Billing and safety (any channel) | Same business day | Owned by the specialist team |
